What is ADHD?

Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD) is a neurodevelopmental condition, meaning it's rooted in how the brain develops rather than in a person's character or effort. It typically becomes noticeable in childhood and, for many people, continues into adulthood. At its core, ADHD involves ongoing patterns of difficulty sustaining attention, acting on impulse, and in some cases feeling restless or overly active, patterns that show up across different settings, like school, work, or home, not just occasionally. It's worth noting that attention itself isn't simply "low" in ADHD; many people with ADHD can focus intensely on tasks they find genuinely engaging, while struggling to engage with tasks that feel repetitive or unrewarding. ADHD looks different from person to person, and it is not a sign of laziness or low intelligence, but a recognized, well-studied condition that can be understood and managed with the right support.

Sources: Developmental Medicine Center, Boston Children's Hospital — Harvard Medical School; MSD Manual, Professional Edition (Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder).
