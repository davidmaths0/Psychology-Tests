What is GAD?

Generalized Anxiety Disorder (GAD) is a mental health condition marked by persistent, excessive worry that is hard to control and that shows up on most days for six months or longer. Unlike the occasional stress everyone experiences before a big event, the worry in GAD tends to attach itself to ordinary, everyday things, work, health, finances, relationships, and often jumps from one concern to the next without a clear trigger. It's usually accompanied by physical symptoms too, like restlessness, muscle tension, fatigue, trouble concentrating, and disrupted sleep, which is part of why many people first bring it up with a primary care doctor rather than a mental health professional. GAD is one of the most common anxiety conditions worldwide, and while it can interfere with daily life if left unaddressed, it responds well to treatment, including therapy, lifestyle changes, and, when needed, medication.

Sources: Harvard Health Publishing, Harvard Medical School (Generalized Anxiety Disorder); Yale School of Medicine, Clinical Keywords (Generalized Anxiety Disorder).
