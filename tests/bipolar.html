What Is Bipolar Disorder?

Bipolar disorder is a mental health condition that can cause distinct episodes of changes in mood, energy, sleep, and activity. These episodes are more intense or last longer than ordinary ups and downs, and they can affect a person's daily life. A manic episode involves an unusually elevated or persistently irritable mood together with increased energy or activity; it can seriously disrupt work, relationships, or safety. Hypomania is a less severe, sustained period of similar changes. Depressive episodes can involve persistent low mood or loss of interest, along with changes in sleep, appetite, energy, concentration, or self-worth.

Bipolar I disorder includes at least one manic episode. Bipolar II disorder involves hypomanic and major depressive episodes, without a manic episode. Cyclothymic disorder involves recurring mood symptoms that do not meet the full criteria for those episodes. A diagnosis depends on a clinician's careful review of a person's history, how long symptoms lasted, how they affected daily life, and possible alternative explanations. A questionnaire can help someone decide whether to seek an evaluation, but it cannot diagnose bipolar disorder.

Sources: National Institute of Mental Health, “Bipolar Disorder”; American Psychiatric Association, DSM-5-TR. This page provides general information, not a diagnosis or medical advice.
