What is Autism?

Autism, or Autism Spectrum Disorder, is a neurodevelopmental condition, meaning it reflects differences in how the brain develops, not a result of upbringing or personal choice. It's called a "spectrum" because it looks different in every person who has it: some autistic traits might be subtle and barely noticeable day to day, while others can meaningfully shape how someone communicates, socializes, or experiences their environment. In general, autism tends to involve differences in social communication and interaction, a strong preference for routine and predictability, deeply focused interests, and, often, heightened sensitivity to sounds, lights, textures, or other sensory input. These aren't necessarily deficits to "fix", many researchers now describe autism through a strengths-based lens, recognizing that traits like intense focus, pattern recognition, and attention to detail can be genuine advantages in the right environment. Autism is usually present from early childhood, though many people, especially women and adults, aren't identified or diagnosed until later in life.

Sources: Boston Children's Hospital, Harvard Medical School (Autism Spectrum Disorders); Stanford Neurodiversity Project, Stanford University School of Medicine (Strengths-Based Model of Neurodiversity).
